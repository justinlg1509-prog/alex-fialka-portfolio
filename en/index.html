Enterprise Thinking

See the whole. Understand the connections. Make better decisions.

Start a conversation Read the book

Most organizations do not suffer from a lack of ideas, technology or talented people.

They struggle because the different parts of the organization no longer work together as one.

Strategy is developed in one place. Processes evolve somewhere else. Technology is introduced to solve individual problems. Departments optimize their own performance. And people are expected to somehow make it all work.

The result is complexity.

Enterprise Thinking is about seeing the organization as a whole.

It helps leaders understand how strategy, organization, processes, information, people and technology influence each other — before deciding what needs to change.

How I Help Companies

I help organizations understand where complexity has developed, why things are not working as expected, and where meaningful improvement can be made.

Not by starting with a new system, a reorganization or another transformation program.

I start by understanding the enterprise.

  • What are you trying to achieve?
  • How does the organization actually work today?
  • Where are the dependencies?
  • Where has unnecessary complexity developed?
  • And what needs to change to make the organization work better as a whole?

From there, we can determine what should be simplified, redesigned, connected or removed.

From Symptoms to Causes

Organizations often try to solve symptoms.

SymptomUsual reaction
A process is too slowso it is automated.
Information is difficult to findso another system is introduced.
Departments do not cooperate effectivelyso the organization is restructured.
Costs are too highso budgets are reduced.

Sometimes these measures help. But sometimes they simply move the problem somewhere else.

Enterprise Thinking asks a different question:

What is causing the problem in the first place?

By looking beyond individual departments, systems and processes, we can identify the relationships behind the problem and create solutions that improve the enterprise rather than only one part of it.

What I Can Help You With

  • Understanding your organization

    Creating a clear picture of how your organization actually works and how strategy, processes, people, information and technology are connected.

  • Reducing complexity

    Identifying unnecessary processes, organizational layers, systems, handovers and dependencies that make the organization harder and more expensive to operate.

  • Improving processes

    Looking beyond individual process steps to understand the complete flow of work and information across organizational boundaries.

  • Aligning business and technology

    Technology should support the organization — not determine how the organization has to work. I help companies understand what they need before deciding which technology they need.

  • Organizational transformation

    Supporting management in determining what really needs to change, what should remain, and how different changes influence each other.

  • Decision support

    Helping leaders see the wider consequences of important decisions before committing significant time, money and organizational capacity.

My Approach

I do not arrive with a predefined solution.

Every organization is different.

The first step is therefore to listen, observe and understand.

We look at the organization from different perspectives and gradually build a shared picture of how the enterprise functions. Then we identify the areas where change can make the greatest difference.

The objective is not to produce the most complicated model.

The objective is to make complexity understandable.

Because when people understand how their organization really works, better decisions become possible.

Enterprise Thinking Before Enterprise Transformation

Transformation has become one of the most frequently used words in business.

  • Digital transformation.
  • Business transformation.
  • Organizational transformation.
  • AI transformation.

But transformation without understanding can become an expensive way of changing the wrong things.

Before transforming an enterprise, we should understand it.

That is the principle behind Enterprise Thinking.

Think first. Understand the whole. Then change what matters.
